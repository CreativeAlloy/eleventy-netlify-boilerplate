It appears that this article type has become its own series on The Wasp Alloy:

- First, we had [Random Vandalism Incidents Attract Disproportionate Attention, Fuel New Antisemitic Rhetoric](https://thewaspalloy.org/posts/random-vandalism-incidents-attract-disproportionate-attention-fuel-new-antisemitic-rhetoric/)
- Then [What in Nirvana Am I Witnessing?](https://thewaspalloy.org/posts/what-in-nirvana-am-i-witnessing/)
- And now, this latest article.

I do not enjoy having to write them, and yet, I can't idly stand by and not address the ridiculously unfair standards that Israel is constantly being subjected to.

In this case, the [Nirvana Fallacy](https://nesslabs.com/nirvana-fallacy) manifests as a collection of unrealistic critiques toward the IDF as an institution, and Jews as a people. They are expected to be all-knowing, flawless, divine groups whose individual members cannot afford to make any mistakes, and when they do, the entire group is blamed for "failing to enforce its own rules."

The purpose of rules is not to prevent accidents from ever happening like magic. Rules exist to be enforced when accidents inevitably happen. The IDF is not a hivemind; individual soldiers from all walks of life are enlisted via mandatory conscription, born out of the undeniable necessity of Jewish and Israeli self-defense against terrorism. It is precisely because private individuals make up a large group that the group can never reasonably guarantee its members will be utterly flawless. It is simply not possible.

When a Bulgarian citizen commits a crime abroad, the police arrest them, and the court analyzes their case to reach a verdict. Because there is no Bulgarian framework which incites criminal activity, the party is acting as a private individual with their own motives. In simple terms, no one can blame Bulgarian society, nor Bulgaria as a nation.

Individual IDF soldiers and Jews are no exception. No group on Earth is exempt - criminals exist everywhere. But when IDF soldiers or Jews carry out the most mundane petty crimes possible, such as a vandalized statue and a guy smoking a cigarette with a sculpture, media coverage is absurdly disproportionate, even from people who claim to be Zionist activists and allies.

**Attempting to smear media coverage misconduct as "impartial, honest reporting" does not absolve it from being Nirvana, nor does it hide the disproportionate public focus.**

In the report which inspired the creation of this very article, a dozen Jews in Judea-Samaria engaged in a five-day civil standoff and skirmish with Arabs. The IDF actively tracked and stopped them from continuing. In a more sane world, that would be the end of it. Not in this one.

Third, ["Palestinians" do not exist, much less "Palestinian-Americans"](https://www.perplexity.ai/search/36fcd4b7-5fc2-4ed7-822d-45b3bfc27ff1) - do not validate a fabricated cult identity. Notice that this American media ambassador called them "Israeli terrorists," and yet not a single person involved has been reported dead. Civil unrest, illegal trespassing, and extremist subcultures exist in every Western democracy. Yet, only in the case of Israel is an isolated civil crime amplified into an existential indictment of the entire nation and its military.

Given how disturbing the rise of antisemitism is in the world, no Zionist activist can afford to place so much focused, disproportionate attention on a series of mundane crimes. As I established in my previous posts, the only reason *I am covering* these events is to expose how distorted people's magnifying glasses are when observing Israel. IDF soldiers, most of whom are teenagers, are put in an incredibly impossible position. Every single minor setback is treated as if an asteroid just hit the entire planet.

No one needs to condone vandalism or random criminal activity. I certainly don't. But when Muslim terrorists and criminals are treated as "just what they always do" (read: they don't receive nearly as much coverage), it implies that they are absolved from condemnation because Muslim culture is subhuman. [That is certainly is, but it doesn't make it any less revolting or worthy of scrutiny.](https://thewaspalloy.org/posts/what-in-nirvana-am-i-witnessing/#cite-3) Nor does it mean that people are free to exaggerate about individual Jewish wrongdoings.

The most deafening part is treating the actual institutional response as a footnote in the entire report. No one seems to put enough emphasis on how amazing the IDF is for stopping the criminal activity in just under five days, considering how volatile the PA-occupied region of Judea-Samaria is. Instead, outlets are more interested in the dozen people that were doing random bullshit just because they happen to be Jewish.

Equating a rogue civil dispute involving a few dozen individuals with organized Jihadi terrorism serves only to launder the atrocities of actual terrorist groups by fallaciously claiming, "See? Both sides commit terror!"

When an ambassador or a media outlet like "A Wider Frame" labels a non-lethal, localized property blockade, and petty civil standoff as "terrorism" on par with Jihadi suicide bombings, rocket barrages, genocides, and massacres, they are flattening the moral landscape to create a false equivalence - either by accident, or on purpose.

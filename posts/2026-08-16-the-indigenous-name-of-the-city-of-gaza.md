## "Gaza" Is Not the Indigenous Name of the City

I found this surprising - after all, even all modern Israelis I have spoken to refer to the city as Gaza, likely due to force of habit.

The truth is that [**the Hebrew name for this city is Azzah.**](https://www.perplexity.ai/search/5b2d8302-00e2-4606-bcb5-c30c379e4119) More specifically, it's the Biblical term. And the reason the other name exists is Philistines.

## The City of Azzah
The erasure of Jewish history is a disturbing and revolting phenomenon. To understand Azzah vs. Gaza, we need to explore the etymological history of the name itself, as well as analyze the Israelite and Greek perspective. This is **Applied Epistemology** in practice, which I will talk about in my future [**Critical Thinking Fundamentals**](https://thewaspalloy.org/posts/critical-thinking-fundamentals-introduction/) lectures.

### The Chronological Angle
Legitimizing the name "Gaza" as indigenous requires ignoring the most critical historical fact in the equation - **[the Philistines were absolutely not the first inhabitants.](https://brewminate.com/ancient-gaza-crossroads-of-cultures-and-empires/)** They were literal colonizers.

[The city of Azzah is over 5000 years old, and it belonged to the Ancient Canaanites (including Israelites)](https://www.albert-tours-israel.com/post/history-of-gaza). It began in the Early Bronze Age as an indigenous Canaanite cultural and agricultural hub with major archaeological sites like Tell es-Sakan. The term "Philistine" (and thus the Roman term "Palestine") originate from the Hebrew "פלשת" - or, the Semitic root "פ-ל-ש," which means **invader.**

Around 1500 BCE, before the Philistines ever arrived, the city fell under ancient Egyptian control and served as an administrative capital. [In ancient Egyptian records and the Amarna letters, it is referred to by its Semitic names: Qazati, Gazzat, and Azzati.](https://lughat.blogspot.com/2006/08/ayin-less-in-gaza.html)

The Philistines only entered the picture during the Bronze Age Collapse. After an invasion from the sea, they conquered the Canaanite coastal lines, and established the pentapolis mentioned earlier.

Given that Canaanites are the first people to be associated with the city, it is important to preserve its indigenous name and refer to it as Azzah. Even though Modern Hebrew has lost the guttural quality of the letter Ayin (ע), it is still the same letter that was used for the name in Hebrew (עַזָה). [A quick Google Translate request reveals this, no less.](https://translate.google.com/?sl=en&tl=iw&text=Gaza&op=translate)

### The Phonetic Angle
The elephant in the room - why was the name change even introduced?

The reason Ancient Greeks renamed the city to Gaza is rather mundane. Because Ancient Greek did not have a letter capturing the exact guttural phonetic profile of the Biblical Hebrew letter Ayin, they simply decided to use Gamma (Γ) as the substitute.

That's all there is to it, but it does not change the fact "Γάζα" is a foreign colonial term, not the indigenous one.
